Past Papers

Where to find past WACE papers for the five subjects

Two sources cover all five subjects: the official SCSA pages (free, with marking keys and formula sheets) and WACE Vault (community-run, with a longer run of years).

SubjectOfficial papers (SCSA)Community vault
MAM · Mathematics MethodsSCSAWACE Vault
HBY · Human BiologySCSAWACE Vault
PHY · PhysicsSCSAWACE Vault
ELD · English (EALD)SCSAWACE Vault
CSC · Computer ScienceSCSAWACE Vault

Between them the two columns cover 2009–2025. The official pages give the paper, the marking key and the formula sheet for each year, starting from 2020; the vault goes further back (2010 for most subjects, 2009 for Computer Science), and for Mathematics Methods it adds a questions-sorted-by-topic folder that beats paging through years when drilling one concept. The exact start and end years differ by subject — see the table above.

Examiner reports: where marks are commonly lost

Each year SCSA publishes an examiner report alongside the paper, setting out where candidates commonly lost marks. Summarised by subject below — the patterns only, no question text.

Subject (report year)Commonly lost marks
MAM · Mathematics Methods
2025
  • Logarithms (4.1)
  • Rectilinear motion and functions defined by an integral (3.1 / 3.2)
  • Confidence intervals computed correctly but not explained (4.3 — flagged three times)
  • Insufficient working on “show that” questions
  • Key features mislocated when sketching graphs
HBY · Human Biology
2025
  • Biotechnology: PCR, gel electrophoresis, protein analysis
  • The Unit 4 extended answer is the weakest part of the paper (42.53% versus 56.73% for Unit 3)
  • Absolute dating (potassium–argon)
  • Gene flow versus genetic drift; founder effect versus speciation
  • Components of a reflex arc
  • Half-life calculations
PHY · Physics
2025
  • Applying trigonometry
  • Algebraic manipulation and separating variables
  • Not planning before writing explanation questions
  • Deriving from first principles
  • Relativistic effects and relativistic momentum/energy (the report calls these widespread misconceptions)
  • Section Three (comprehension and data analysis) is the weakest of the three sections
ELD · English (EALD)
2025 + 2024 + 2023
  • Synthesis that only summarises without combining sources (weak three years running)
  • Submitting a memorised essay that does not answer the question
  • Formulaic “firstly / secondly / thirdly”
  • Writing a speech as though it were an essay
  • Leaving parts of multi-part questions unanswered
  • Poor time allocation
CSC · Computer Science
2025
  • Subnetting and IP addressing (including router interfaces)
  • Interpreting network performance data
  • SQL joins
  • Multi-step algorithms: 2-D arrays, dictionaries, sorting, object-oriented code
  • Applying encryption concepts (recognition is fine; contextual explanation is not)
  • Struggling to break down low-scaffold scenario questions

Source: the examiner's report SCSA publishes alongside each year's paper (ATAR course examination report) — the document the Authority issues precisely to tell teachers and students where marks are commonly lost. This is a categorised summary of it and contains no question text. Reports are written year by year, so an item marked 2025 comes from the 2025 report and is not a claim that it recurs annually; the ELD row merges three reports because the 2025 one runs only two pages.

The “community” column is a third-party collection, not an official source. It was found to carry commercial textbook PDFs and internal school test papers alongside past exams. This site neither hosts nor endorses that material; the link is an entry point only. If you want to stay clear of it, use the SCSA pages above — free, no login, 2020 onwards.
