Glossary

Terms and abbreviations used across this site, previously left unexplained

This page defines terms only; it makes no predictions. Figures this site cannot verify — your own intake's conversion curve, for instance — are deliberately left out. Items marked with a source can be checked on the issuing body's website.

The five subjects

MAM
Mathematics Methods		Mathematics Methods — calculus and statistics.
Source: this site's Subjects page
HBY
Human Biology		Human Biology.
Source: this site's Subjects page
PHY
Physics		Physics.
Source: this site's Subjects page
ELD
English as an Additional Language or Dialect (EAL/D)		English as an Additional Language or Dialect (EAL/D). Not the same as the English ATAR course, which is for first-language speakers; the Past Papers page flags that distinction.
Source: SCSA
CSC
Computer Science		Computer Science.
Source: this site's Subjects page
AUSMAT
Australian Matriculation		Australian Matriculation: a pre-university programme taught in Malaysia that sits the Western Australian WACE / ATAR. This site documents one intake of it.
Source: SCSA and general course knowledge

Admissions

ATAR
Australian Tertiary Admission Rank		A rank between 0 and 99.95 — not a mark and not a percentage. It states your position within your cohort: an ATAR of 80 means you are in the top 20%. Universities admit on this number.
Source: TISC, Tertiary Entrance Aggregate and ATAR
TEA
Tertiary Entrance Aggregate		The aggregate: the scaled marks of your best four ATAR subjects, summed, plus any bonus that applies. TISC publishes a maximum of 430. The ATAR is derived from it, so the aggregate is an intermediate value, not the outcome.
Source: TISC admission guides (the 2027–2029 editions all state the 430 maximum)
Scaling
Scaled marks		Marks adjusted for course difficulty. The ATAR uses scaled marks, not the raw marks on a school report — so 80 in Mathematics and 80 in English do not contribute equally. This site's estimate uses one institution's curve for one intake; it is not the official conversion.
Source: TISC documentation
AT course
ATAR course		The course type that counts toward an ATAR. WACE also has General and Foundation courses, whose marks do not count toward an ATAR. This site records AT subjects only.
Source: SCSA, WACE Manual
Mathematics bonus
Mathematics bonus		Studying and examining in Mathematics Methods and/or Mathematics Specialist adds 10% of that subject's scaled score to the best-four aggregate — regardless of whether the subject is one of the best four. The same guide states the maximum TEA is 430.
Source: TISC, Steps to uni for Year 12 students — stated identically in the 2027, 2028 and 2029 editions
Note: A February 2024 news report said bonuses like this would be scrapped, and that report was widely repeated. TISC's own guides for 2027–2029 entry still state the bonus applies, so this site follows TISC's own wording.
WACE
Western Australian Certificate of Education		The Western Australian Certificate of Education. Earning it and earning an ATAR are two separate things: graduation has its own credit and literacy/numeracy requirements (see OLNA).
Source: SCSA
SCSA
School Curriculum and Standards Authority		The Western Australian curriculum and standards authority: it writes the syllabuses, sets the external exams, and publishes marking keys and examiner reports. Every official paper linked from this site comes from its website, scsa.wa.edu.au (often mis-cited online as scsa.wa.gov.au, which does not exist).
Source: SCSA
TISC
Tertiary Institutions Service Centre		The body that calculates the ATAR and handles university applications and offers in Western Australia. The ATAR is issued by TISC, not by your school.
Source: TISC
OLNA
Online Literacy and Numeracy Assessment		The literacy and numeracy assessment required to graduate with a WACE — separate from the ATAR. You need Level 3 in reading, writing and numeracy; resits are available. This site's timeline does not carry its dates; check with the school.
Source: SCSA

Assessment task abbreviations

TT
Topic Test		A staged test, usually covering one to a few chapters. Weighting varies by subject; it is the most frequent scored item during the semester.
Source: Subject assessment outlines
EE
Evaluation Exam		An evaluation exam held during the course, usually at the end of semester 1 or the start of semester 2. Not the same as FE, which comes at the end.
Source: PHY and HBY assessment outlines
EA
Evaluation & Analysis		Physics' evaluation-and-analysis task, weighted 12%.
Source: PHY assessment outline
FE
Final Evaluation / Final Examination		The final exam, usually covering the whole course (Units 3 and 4). Normally the heaviest single item in a subject.
Source: Subject assessment outlines
ER
Extended Response		A long-answer or essay-style task. Human Biology uses it to assess set chapters.
Source: HBY assessment outline
Synthesis
Combining sources, not summarising		Combining several sources into one piece, rather than summarising each in turn. It is part of EAL/D Section Two, and the examiner reports have flagged it as weak three years running: candidates summarise — one paragraph per source, with no connection between them. The passing move is to make the sources talk to each other: which supports which, where they conflict, and what the combination implies.
Source: SCSA EAL/D examiner reports and examination design brief
COAT
Common Oral Assessment Task		An oral task set by SCSA and administered by the school (ELD Task 3). You get five minutes to view an image and make notes, then a ten-minute interview; the recording goes to SCSA.
Source: ELD assessment outline
Readiness taskA diagnostic test at the start of the course. The two recorded here are unweighted and serve to establish a baseline.
Source: MAM assessment outline
InvestigationAn investigation: you pose a question, research it and report. In Mathematics Methods it runs in two parts (plan, then in-class validation); in ELD it is an oral proposal (Part A) plus a written report (Part B).
Source: MAM and ELD assessment outlines
ACCT
Internal school assessment code (no published expansion)		An internal code the school uses in its calendar: a 60-minute test held alongside EE. Its full form is not published anywhere this site could find, and it is not invented here. Whether it counts toward your grade is for the school to confirm — the course materials record it as unconfirmed.
Source: Course materials, where it appears as a code only
CAS
Computer Algebra System		A calculator that does symbolic algebra, not just numbers: differentiation, integration, solving equations, simplification. The MAM external exam has two sections — Section One bans all calculators (50 minutes, 35%), Section Two allows a graphics/CAS calculator (100 minutes, 65%, two A4 sheets of handwritten notes permitted, formula sheet supplied). Where the mind map says “use CAS”, it means Section Two.
Source: MAM course and assessment outline
Lab AssessmentAssessment of practical skills, drawn from the experiments done during the course. In Physics it has a practical component and a written one.
Source: PHY and HBY assessment outlines
