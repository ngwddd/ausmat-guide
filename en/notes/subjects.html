Five Subjects

Assessment structure and content covered

For each subject below: how the marks are made up, and what content each assessment covers.

MAM · Mathematics Methods

Weighting: Tests 34% + Investigation 26% + Examination 40% = 100%

AssessmentContent covered
Topic Tests 1–3Further differentiation and applications: exponential and trigonometric rules, the second derivative and its applications
InvestigationPlan, research, conduct and communicate findings; in-class validation
Final ExaminationAll Unit 3 and Unit 4 content, including integrals, the logarithmic function, discrete and continuous random variables, the normal distribution, and interval estimates for proportions

Note that Topic Test 2 alone is worth 20% — more than either Investigation — and it falls inside the November examination window.

PHY · Physics

Weighting: Tests 40% + Examination 50% + Science Inquiry 10% = 100%

AssessmentContent covered
Topic Test 1 / 2Unit content as taught to that point, assessed through problems and calculations
Evaluation & AnalysisExtended evaluation and analysis of a physical situation
Lab AssessmentScience Inquiry: practical work plus a written component
Evaluation Exam / Final EvaluationCumulative examination; the final covers all of Units 3 and 4

Note that the two Topic Tests total 28%, not much less than the 30% final. Focusing only on the final leaves nearly a third of the marks exposed. This outline states no specific dates.

CSC · Computer Science

Weighting: Project 40% + Examination 40% + Theory test 10% + Practical Test 10% = 100%

AssessmentContent covered
Task 1 (Project)Object-oriented program for a business client: two-dimensional array, classes, control structures; design and development, evaluation, project validation
Task 2 (Practical Test)A series of programming items of increasing difficulty, completed on a computer
Task 4 (Theory Test)Network communications and cyber security, including network diagrams using CISCO conventions, addressing, subnets, segmentation and security
Task 5 (Project)Integrated database programming solution; relational database design and SQL queries, including referential integrity, insert/delete, inner joins, calculated fields
Task 3 / Task 6 (Examination)Three-hour examinations using the ATAR Year 12 examination design brief

The two projects total 40%, equal to the two examinations combined, and each runs over several weeks. These most reward starting early.

ELD · English (EALD)

Weighting: Written production 30% + Oral production 10% + Response 15% + Investigation 15% + Final examination 30% = 100%

AssessmentContent covered
Task 1 / Task 6Written production: essay and persuasive speech, responding to previously unseen questions
Task 2A / 2BInvestigation: oral research proposal, then a written research report
Task 3 (COAT)Common Oral Assessment Task set by SCSA and administered by the school
Task 4 / Task 5Response: listening comprehension, and reading comprehension with synthesis writing
Task 7Oral production: persuasive speech delivered to peers
Task 8Final written examination

Eight tasks, the most of any subject. Spoken tasks exceed 20% combined. Recording yourself and listening back helps: most people cannot hear their own pace or filler pauses while speaking.

HBY · Human Biology

No verifiable assessment data was found for this subject, so neither weightings nor scope are listed — better to leave a gap than to fill it with guesswork.

When revising, start with the content-covered column: it tells you which topics each assessment draws on, which is more efficient than re-reading the whole course.
